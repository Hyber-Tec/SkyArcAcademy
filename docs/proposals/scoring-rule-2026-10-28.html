How a shot on a line is scored, and what the app does

How competitions score a shot that sits on a ring’s line, what the academy’s coaches do on practice cards, and what Goo decided for the app’s card reader. The academy’s result sheets and cards stay on our computer: every picture here is drawn, with made-up shots, and the numbers are totals.

Decided (Goo, October 28)

Option A, the matches’ rule: a hole touching a ring’s line counts that ring. One change: the reader measures every hole at its real size in the photo, instead of taking it at the pellet’s 4.5 mm. The manager isn’t asked, and “most of the hole” is dropped. Built in #68 (section 7).

The short answer

  • In matches, touching the line counts. A machine measures every shot; when the pellet’s hole touches a ring’s line, even barely, the shot gets that ring. All eight result sheets we were given follow this.
  • “Most of the hole” isn’t a match rule. On those sheets it would have taken about 50 points off every three-position score.
  • The coaches score practice cards a little more strictly: a hole that only grazes a line usually gets the lower ring.
  • The hole in the card is smaller than the pellet (about 4.2 mm, not 4.5), so the app now measures each hole and scores it at that size.

Made up, laid out as the sheets print it.

  • Each shot gets a score with one decimal, like 8.4 or 10.2: the machine measures where the pellet went.
  • In three-position matches the decimal is simply dropped: 9.9 counts as 9, never 10. The series above adds up to 85.
  • A star marks an inner ten: 10.2 or better, when the pellet takes out the whole centre dot. 10.1 has none. The number after the dash counts them; they break ties.
  • In 60-shot matches the decimals are added up, for totals like 587.4.
  • We checked every 10-shot series on all eight sheets (479 shots): every total is exactly what these rules give.

2Touching the line counts

The decimals are distances. 10.9 is the dead centre, and each tenth less is a quarter of a millimetre further out. The whole number changes exactly where the pellet’s hole, 4.5 mm across, touches a ring’s line:

To be sure this holds for every shot, we measured the shot circles on the sheets’ target pictures: 220 shots on 22 pictures (the others were too crowded to tell the circles apart). Every printed score matched “the highest ring the pellet’s hole touches” to within one or two tenths.

3“Most of the hole” would lower every match score

Scored by “most of the hole”, about 8 shots in 10 on these sheets would lose a point: the five three-position scores would have dropped by 44 to 54 points each, and the 60-shot ones, counted in whole numbers, by 38 to 51. A practice score kept that way would read about 8 points per 10 shots below what the same shots score in a match.

So the coaches are close to the match rule, but a hole has to clearly cut into the line, about ¾ mm, before they give the higher ring. That’s natural when scoring by eye: a hole in paper is a little smaller than the pellet that made it, and the printed lines have their own width. It isn’t “most of the hole”: they give the higher ring long before most of the hole is past the line.

6The decision: the match rule, with the hole as it really is

Goo chose A: practice cards are scored the way every match the students shoot is scored, so a practice 90 means what a match 90 means. Looking at the grazing hole above, Goo added one change: the circle the app drew for each hole was the pellet’s 4.5 mm, bigger than the hole the pellet leaves in the card, so it touched lines the real hole doesn’t, where the coaches saw the lower ring. The app now measures each hole and applies the rule to that.

7What changed (#68)

  • The reader measures each hole: from its centre it looks along 48 directions for where the card’s print starts again (halfway down that last step, so a blurry photo or a shadow inside the hole doesn’t move it), and fits a circle to those points: the hole’s size, and a better centre.
  • When a hole can’t be read cleanly (too much of its edge on the printed lines or under ink, a big tear, two holes that touch), it takes the middle of the card’s other holes, and the pellet’s size if fewer than three could be read. Nothing is fitted to the academy’s cards: each card is measured on its own.
  • The card’s page on the website draws each hole at its size and says how big it is (“hole 4.2 mm across”). A hole whose edge only just reaches a line is still marked for a look before saving. The phone app follows with its next update.
  • Cards saved before keep their scores.
